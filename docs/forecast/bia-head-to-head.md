# Next-word and fill-in-the-blank guessers: head-to-head

Generated by `pnpm bia:head-to-head` (tables) and `pnpm bia:explain` (the worked examples), 2026-10-12.
Test split: every 10th verse of each English-source parallel pair is held out. A project of size N trains
on its first N translated verse pairs and is scored on the held-out verses of the first 2,000-verse span
(219–223 verses, the same verses for every N ≤ 2,000). "full" trains on every pair and scores every
held-out verse. Each task scores 1,500 positions. Results are top-1 / top-3 exact-word accuracy (%).
CIs come from a paired bootstrap that resamples whole test verses (2,000 resamples), so positions inside
one verse are not treated as independent.

## Ten target languages

- **Low-resource (the tuning targets):** Nepali, Tok Pisin, K'iche', Chuukese.
- **Scripts without spaces between words:** Thai, Burmese, Japanese.
- **Agglutinative or polysynthetic:** Quechua, Turkish, Inupiatun.

## Columns

| Column | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **BIA** | The ported Codex Editor BIA vote with the bigram weight. |
| **V6 (2024-09)** | An eval-only reconstruction of Codex Editor's 2024-09 TypeScript forecaster (c42263ea): ±1 bigrams over the whole corpus as one stream, plus similar words. Next word only. |
| **chains** | Per-distance chains: the words 1–3 places away, combined as a product of experts. For next word it reads the last 3 words forward. For infill it also reads the next 3 words backward, so it is bidirectional. |
| **BIA + chains** | The BIA vote, weighted by the chains instead of the bigram. |
| **source** | BIA plus the project-learned source lexicon, with the bigram weight. |
| **source + chains** | BIA plus the source lexicon, weighted by the chains. |
| **source + chains-only** | Only the chains' candidates plus the source lexicon, weighted by the chains. |
| **BIA src+tgt corpus** | Plain BIA trained on the training source verses and target verses as one unaligned corpus. This is the spring-2024 Codex Editor setup (complete_draft.txt, 2024-03-23 to 2024-04-29), the only time an old forecaster saw the source. |
| **default** | What the editor now does: `chooseGuesser` in `src/lib/forecast/bia-engine.ts`. |

## The default (`chooseGuesser`)

The rule uses only signals the app has at runtime: the task, whether the cell has a source verse, and how
many translated verse pairs the project has. It knows nothing about the language.

| task | no source verse (or no translated pairs) | source, < 1,000 translated pairs | source, ≥ 1,000 translated pairs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| next word | chains only | BIA + source, bigram-weighted | BIA + source, bigram-weighted |
| fill in the blank | BIA | **BIA** (was BIA + source) | BIA + source |

**What changed: one cell.** Fill-in-the-blank no longer uses the source until the project has 1,000
translated pairs.

- **Below 1,000 pairs:** source-aware infill loses to plain BIA at 100 pairs (mean −0.9; 4 of 10
  languages really worse, none better) and is a wash at 250.
- **From 1,000 pairs:** it wins in 8–9 of 10 languages, with none worse. The switch happens between
  500 and 1,000 pairs in every language.
- **Cost of a single threshold:** infill at 500–999 pairs forgoes about 0.5 points (really worse in 4 of
  10 languages at 500). Moving the infill threshold to 500 would recover that. It is a 0.09-point gain
  averaged over all languages and sizes, within noise, so one constant is kept.

**Measured, better on average, but not made the default: chain-weighting the next-word source mix below
about 1,000 pairs** (*source + chains*).

- **The gain:** at 100 pairs it beats the shipped bigram-weighted mix in 10 of 10 languages (+3.2
  top-1). At 250 pairs it beats it in 9 of 10 (+2.1), and at 500 in 4 of 10 (+1.4). From 1,000 pairs it
  is a wash.
- **The cost:** the chain product multiplies every candidate by the probability of its slot. A word only
  the source proposes, one the target context never predicts, therefore gets the chain floor (ε·P(w))
  and can no longer surface mid-sentence. The source exists to supply exactly such words.
- **The evidence:** the ghost-text journey (`e2e/specs/editor/ghost-text.spec.ts`) fails under it.
  "Y Dios los bendijo diciendo:" no longer offers *Fructificad* ("Be fruitful"), a word no earlier verse
  puts there.
- **A fix that failed:** adding the source after the chain weight instead of before it collapsed
  accuracy to source-only levels (1–5 % top-1). The mixing weight is tuned for BIA votes, not chain
  probabilities.
- **What it would take:** a dev-split retune of that mixing weight. Until then this is a product choice
  for Daniel: higher average next-word accuracy in young projects, or key terms from the source. The
  *source + chains* column and the `source + chains − default` comparison show the size of the trade.

## How the source-aware guess works: a real example

This trace is printed by `pnpm bia:explain`. The values come from the engine's own functions. The anchor
list comes from instrumenting the real posting-list lookups. The final ranking is checked against
`BiaEngine.suggestNext`.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Eval set | English → Tok Pisin, 2,000 translated pairs |
| Held-out verse | Genesis 1:10 |
| Source verse | God called the dry land "earth", and the gathering together of the waters he called "seas". God saw that it was good. |
| Target | Dispela ples **drai** God i kolim "Graun." … |
| Typed so far | "dispela ples". The next word is **drai**. |

1. **Target side (BIA).**
   - **Anchors:** the words already typed, rarest first: *ples* (1 back, IDF 3.38) and *dispela* (2 back, IDF 1.92).
   - **Lookup:** in every validated verse containing an anchor, read the word the same distance away.
   - **Result:** five words tie at 1.5 votes (*i, na, bilong, ol, em*). *drai* is further down. Plain BIA,
     after its bigram weight, says *bilong, na, drai*, so it gets this one wrong.
2. **Source side (the lexicon).**
   - **Cursor:** the project's target/source length ratio is 1.37, so the target should be about 30.2 words
     long. The cursor sits at relative position 0.083.
   - **Scoring:** every source word contributes IDF(source word) × Dice(source word, target word)² × a
     position weight that falls off with the distance between the source word's relative position and the cursor's.
   - **For *drai*, "dry" carries it.**
     - Verse counts: 7 verses have "dry" in the source and "drai" in the target, 2 have "dry" only, 19
       have "drai" only (e.g. GEN 1:9, 8:13, 8:14).
     - Dice = 2·7 / (9 + 26) = 0.400.
     - "dry" is word 4 of 22, near the cursor, so its position weight is 0.543.
     - With IDF 6.30, that gives 0.547. *drai*'s total is 0.551.
   - **For *bilong*, "the" carries it.**
     - Verse counts: 1,229 verses have both, so Dice is 0.787, but IDF is only 1.26.
     - *bilong*'s total is 1.091, higher than *drai*'s at this step.
3. **× target-word IDF³.** This lets the source speak for content words and leaves function words to the
   context. *drai* (IDF 5.31) becomes the top source score at 82.3. *bilong* (IDF 1.23) drops out.
4. **Mix.** The mix is BIA/max(BIA) + 2 × source/max(source). *drai* scores 3.00, *god* 1.96.
5. **Bigram weight** × (1 + ln(1 + count(*ples* → w))). The count of *ples* → *drai* is 17, and of *ples*
   → *bilong* is 48. The final ranking is *drai* 11.7, *god* 5.5, *bilong* 5.1. Source-aware gets it right.

**Where it gets it wrong.** Same verse, typed "dispela ples drai god i kolim graun". The next word is
*na*, and plain BIA gets it.

- **Where *na* comes from:** its source score comes from "and" (Dice 0.821) and "the". But *na* is a
  function word (IDF 1.11), so the IDF³ step shrinks it.
- **What wins instead:** *kenan* (Canaan, IDF high), linked to "land" (48 verses both, Dice 0.323, "land"
  near the cursor). It outranks *na* after the mix and the bigram weight.
- **Final ranking:** *kenan, bilong, na*.
- **The lesson:** the IDF³ step that rescued *drai* also lets a strongly associated rare word override a
  common one that the context alone would have guessed.
- **A related oddity:** numbers that occur in only a few verses (*601*, *27*) also reach the top of the
  source list after IDF³.

## BIA and the per-distance chains: related, not the same

Both answer "which word is found d places from this context word in other verses?". They differ in four ways:

| | BIA (`predictAt`) | per-distance chains (`chainProbability`, `backChainProbability`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context used | The 15 rarest words of the verse, on both sides of the blank, at any distance | The nearest 3 words before (and, for infill, after) the blank, whatever their rarity |
| What is counted | At query time, for each anchor: one vote per distinct word it lands on (presence), decayed 1/\|d\| | Precomputed full counts of the word k places after/before each word → probabilities P_k(w \| context word) |
| Combination | Votes are summed, then × bigram factor 1 + ln(1 + count) | Product of experts: Π_k (P_k + ε·P(w))^(1/k), so a word never seen in that slot is pushed far down |
| Fills a gap from both sides | Yes (right-side anchors count backwards) | Before this change: no (forward only, next word only). Now: yes (backward chains) |

**Are the ports faithful?**

- **BIA:** a faithful port of `servers/utils/bia.py`, with the deviations documented in `bia-engine.ts`.
  `FAITHFUL_OPTIONS` reproduces the Python. It scores far lower in `bia-eval-results.md`, which is why it
  is not shipped.
- **Chains:** not a port of one Codex Editor version. The per-distance, forward-only lookup is the first
  Python forecaster's (TextGenerator, 8f8f4a41, 2024-03). The 2024-09 TypeScript forecaster (V6,
  c42263ea) used only adjacent ±1 forward/backward bigrams. Its reconstruction is the **V6** column.

**Combining them.** BIA + chains was already possible (`chains: true, chainsOnly: false`), and it is
measured here (*BIA + chains*, *source + chains*). Without a source it ties the chains for next word.
With a source it wins below 1,000 pairs but floors source-only words (see "The default"). No default
uses it.

**Why the chains sat behind an option (aea4bf8b9).** They were never a user setting. They were on
automatically for next word without a source signal and off with one.

- **The tuning that decided it** covered only the first-2,000-verse dev splits (about 1,800 training
  pairs). There the source path scored 38.48 with the bigram, 38.42 with chains only and 38.39 with
  chains (`bia-chains-dev-early.md`, 400 positions): a 0.1-point difference, i.e. noise.
- **Infill had no chain path** because the chains were forward only.
- **What this head-to-head adds:** it covers smaller projects. There the chain weighting wins on
  average, but it costs source key terms (see above).
- **The default is unchanged:** chains only for next word without a source (best or tied there at every
  size), and the bigram-weighted mix with a source.

**Bidirectional chains for infill (restored here).** They are now in the engine. They **lose** to BIA
for infill at every size: −0.8 at 100 pairs to −2.9 on full Bibles, and really worse in 3–9 of 10
languages per size. They stay an option and are not the default.

## Language-agnosticism check (from the code)

| | BIA | chains | source-aware |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Word boundaries | Shared tokenizer (`forecast-tokenize.ts`): NFC, `Intl.Segmenter`, Unicode classes; no whitespace assumption | Same | Same, on both source and target |
| Casing | Locale-independent folding; uncased scripts untouched | Same | Same |
| Affixes / morphology | Whole word forms only (sparser in agglutinative languages) | Whole word forms; a 3-word context is sparser still | Whole target forms; the stem back-off exists but is off |
| Word order | Target-only, any distance, both sides | Target-only, fixed window | Dice is order-free, but the position weight assumes roughly monotone source→target order |
| RTL | Logical order; nothing special | Same | Same |
| Data needed | Works from the first validated verse | Same | Needs source text and translated pairs: hurts infill below about 500 pairs and helps from about 1,000 in every language tested |

**Measured.**

- **Spaceless scripts:** Thai, Burmese and Japanese behave like the other languages. Source infill helps
  from 500–1,000 pairs. On full Bibles the source is the best next-word guesser for Thai (+5.6 over BIA).
- **Burmese segmentation:** ICU's `Intl.Segmenter` splits Burmese into syllables, not words (e.g. ဘု | ရား
  for "God"). Every method therefore suggests syllables in Burmese.
- **Agglutinative / polysynthetic:** accuracy is low for every method (Quechua 3–14 % next word,
  Inupiatun 5–18 %). The source helps later and less.
  - Quechua infill: −1.1 to −1.7 at ≤ 500 pairs (real), a tie at 1,000, +1.5 at 2,000.
  - Turkish: the source is the clear winner on full Bibles (+2.7 next word, +5.4 infill over BIA).
- **The pair-count gate:** below 1,000 pairs it removes the source's early infill losses in all three
  groups. One exception: at 500–999 pairs it forgoes about 0.5 points (see the `default − source` infill row).

**The trade-off.**

- BIA and the chains are the most language-agnostic by construction. They need only target text, make no
  word-order assumption across languages, and work from the first verse.
- The source lexicon assumes paired data and roughly monotone order, but it is the most accurate once a
  project has about 1,000 pairs. That held in all 10 languages, including the SOV, spaceless and
  agglutinative ones.
- For infill, the default gates the source on a language-independent signal (the pair count). It uses
  plain BIA while BIA is better and the source when the source is better, without knowing the language.
- For next word in projects under about 1,000 pairs, the most accurate options are the agnostic ones:
  - Chains only beats the bigram-weighted source mix by 2.5 points at 100 pairs (7 of 10 languages
    really better) and by 1.5 at 250.
  - *Source + chains* is better still.
  - But both suppress source key terms, and the chains-only path cannot suggest a first word for an
    empty cell.
  - So the default keeps the source mix there. **This is the open decision:** accuracy in young projects
    (the agnostic choice), or key terms from the source.

## The measurement (tokenizer) is the one users get

- **One tokenizer:** the eval scores against `wordKeys` from `forecast-tokenize.ts`. The index (`segmentWords`),
  the caret parser in `BiaEngine`, the source lexicon and the thesaurus all use that same module, so the
  eval measures the tokens users get.
- **No method tokenizes differently:** every column runs through the same `fill()` path and is scored
  against the same keys.
- **One caveat, shared by all methods:** the eval types the left context as space-joined keys. In a
  spaceless script it therefore never exercises the runtime rule "a caret right after a complete known
  word is a boundary".
- **Nepali, current code with only the tokenizer swapped** (old regex `[\p{L}\p{N}\p{M}]+`, which splits
  at ZWJ → new segmenter):

## Caveats

- The eval sets are frozen and deterministic. A rerun gives identical numbers (verified: 900/900 cells
  identical across two runs). The only spread is between languages and sizes; there is no run-to-run
  variance.
- The CIs cover sampling of test verses within one Bible. They do not cover other translations of the
  same language, other genres, or a team's real typing (prefix completion, partially validated text).
- Sizes between the tested points (e.g. 600 or 1,500 pairs) are interpolated; the threshold could sit
  anywhere between 500 and 2,000 with little change.
- The source in every pair is English (WEB). Other source languages are untested.
